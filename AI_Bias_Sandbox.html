Methodology and source bridge

Rubric version 2.0 • Transparent, deterministic, and designed for scenario comparison

What the scores mean

Exposure is a weighted average of eight risk-mode scores. Reliability and governance are separate control-readiness proxies. Inputs make additive adjustments to task-specific baselines, then decision impact applies a 0.85, 1.00, or 1.18 multiplier to exposure. Results are clamped to 0–100.

Interpretation bands

  • 0–29: Low
  • 30–59: Moderate
  • 60–100: High

The same numerical bands are used for all three scores, but direction differs: lower exposure is better; higher reliability and governance are better.

Coefficient map and sensitivity

The rubric is intentionally inspectable. The largest individual exposure adjustments are shown below so reviewers can challenge the assumptions instead of treating the output as a black box.

View coefficient highlights
  • Task baselines range from 10 to 36 points across the eight axes.
  • Low representativeness can add up to 30 Representation points and 20 Coverage points. Low label quality can add up to 28 Measurement points.
  • A prior-value anchor adds 30 Anchoring points and 8 Confirmation points. A must-fit constraint adds 22 Confirmation points, 8 Anchoring points, and 6 Automation Bias points.
  • No grounding adds 14 Confabulation points and 6 Overconfidence points. Tools plus verification subtract 14 and 6 points, respectively.
  • No required human review adds 18 Automation Bias points and 8 Overconfidence points. Automation level can add up to 26 and 10 points, respectively.
  • Axis weights range from 1.05 to 1.20, with Confabulation receiving the highest weight. Decision impact multiplies exposure by 0.85, 1.00, or 1.18.

Terminology bridge

The radar deliberately combines bias pathways (representation, selection, measurement, anchoring, confirmation, and automation bias) with generative-AI failure modes (confabulation and overconfidence). The heading says “bias and failure-mode profile” so hallucination is not mislabeled as a bias category.

Limitations

  • Coefficients are expert-designed training heuristics, not empirically calibrated probabilities.
  • The tool does not certify fairness, validate a model, replace independent review, evaluate people, or generate cost values.
  • Use before and after comparisons as evidence that controls were considered, not as proof that residual risk is eliminated.
  • Context still governs. A “low” result can be unacceptable for a consequential use case with weak evidence.

Four-step playbook

Use the sandbox early, before AI-generated assumptions become estimate inputs.

1. Map the actual use

Select the AI task and decision impact. Describe the evidence, prompt, review process, and operating environment as they exist today.

2. Read the shape, not just the headline

Use the radar and driver list to identify the likely pathway. An overall score can hide a concentrated anchoring or confabulation problem.

3. Apply and test controls

Use the recommended control cards to simulate one change at a time. Compare the before and after profile and record the residual risk.

4. Carry the result into the estimate

Translate the top drivers, mitigations, and limitations into GR&A, peer-review notes, a Model Card, or a CARD addendum. Preserve human accountability for the decision.
